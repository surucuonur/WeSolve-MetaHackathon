## What it does

This territory is not code in the decode step. It is the measurement that decides whether round 3's attempts actually made the model faster: Claude's G3 of the routing+combine pair (routing attempt-6 + combine attempt-2) alone and together, the swarm's ready check of the same pair, the one-request long-prompt run, and the experts attempt-1 piece result that never reached G3, all written up in [claude/findings/muse-round-3.md](../../../claude/findings/muse-round-3.md). Raw data in `results/runs/2026-11-04-muse-round-3/`, `results/runs/2026-11-04-muse-round-3-long/` and `muse-spark/round-3/checks/ready-1/`. Frozen prior work: round 3's [territory-g3.md](../../round-3/exploration/territory-g3.md) and [ineff-g3-1.md](../../round-3/exploration/ineff-g3-1.md)–[ineff-g3-3.md](../../round-3/exploration/ineff-g3-3.md) (cited, not repeated). Its questions for round 4 are where the end-to-end change differs from the piece timing now, what a set containing an experts attempt with `min_rows: 2` would predict at the four G3 workloads, and whether the rounds 1–2 three-slot interaction still stands in the way.

Every speed number below says its level and kind: **measured, piece** (grader `grade.json`, chained µs per layer), **measured, whole request** (ready check / G3, vLLM, 5 shuffled repeats, 256 generated tokens), or **prediction** (arithmetic shown, counting only the candidate's own measured effect). A combination is measured as one, never added up from its slots; sums of piece effects are labelled predictions.

## Where the end-to-end change differs from the piece timing, and why

Two gaps are live for round 4. A third and fourth round-3 gap are closed and recorded so nobody re-chases them.

1. **The pair at 32 rows, above all 32×1024: piece says −0.656 ms/step vs the compiled reference, G3 keeps −0.548 (32×128) and −0.362 (32×1024) in Claude's run.** Shortfall (G3 − piece, vs compiled): −0.042 ms/step at 1×128 (G3 beats the piece sum), +0.015 at 8×128, +0.108 at 32×128, **+0.294 at 32×1024** (+14.7 µs/layer; vs the uncompiled reference the 32×1024 shortfall is +0.331: piece −0.976 vs measured −0.645). In the swarm's run the same 32×1024 shortfall is only +0.068 (−0.588 measured vs −0.656 piece), so 0.23 ms/step of the Claude shortfall is run-to-run movement, the same order as the shortfall itself. What is solid in both runs: the median gap vs `compiled-all` at 32×1024 is 0.9% (Claude) / 1.5% (swarm) and the 5-repeat ranges overlap in Claude's run ([38.589–39.080] vs [38.912–39.400]) and miss in the swarm's — the verdict flips on the draw. Piece 4 rules out prompt length alone (at 1 row the piece sum transfers fully at 1,024 and 7,936 tokens: −0.428/−0.487 measured vs −0.394 piece), and piece 3 rules out token divergence and kernel count. The residual grows with decode rows (1→32) and again with context at fixed 32 rows (30.1→39.4 ms steps), while the slots' own cost is context-independent — consistent with a fixed in-step composition cost the chained timing cannot see, at a size (≤0.3 ms/step) that 5 repeats cannot separate from machine drift (swap 13.9–14.2 GB). Not explained beyond that; posted as [ineff-g3-1](ineff-g3-1.md) (open). The practical consequence is a margin requirement, not a correctness one: the set needs more than ~1% at 32×1024, or more repeats than G3 gives it.
2. **All three together at 8 requests: untested since round 2, and it decides the `min_rows` set.** The additive prediction for the set (next section) is −2.16 ms/step vs base at 8×128; the historical three-way term is +2.13 to +2.86 ms/step at exactly that workload (piece 6). If it recurs at even its smallest measured size, the set's 8×128 decode gain is gone (−2.158+2.128 = −0.030 ms/step); at its largest the set is slower than base (+0.70). At 32 requests the historical term (+0.19) barely dents the prediction (−7.40→−7.21 vs base). No new run constrains this: round 3 timed no experts variant end to end. Posted as [ineff-g3-2](ineff-g3-2.md) (open); the ready check, which times candidates alone and all together, is the first instrument that will see it in round 4.
3. **Closed — combine at 32 requests (round 3's gap 1).** The +1.05/+1.17 ms/step in-step penalty of combine attempt-1 (volatile barriers; round 3's [ineff-g3-1.md](../../round-3/exploration/ineff-g3-1.md), confirmed, mechanism owned by `baseline-combine`) does not appear with attempt-2: its G3 medians favour the kernel at every workload in both runs (piece 3 singles: −0.555/−0.511/−0.453/−0.484 vs base; piece 2: −0.53/−0.59/−0.44/−0.26), and the pair's remaining 32-row shortfall (gap 1 above) is 4–5× smaller and inside the spread. Nothing to re-post.
4. **Closed — prompts (round 3's gap 3).** Decode-only predictions no longer overstate whole requests: with routing handed off above 32 rows and combine faster at every prefill size (piece 1), the pair's prompts are 1.6–3.1% faster than base and slower nowhere, and whole requests track decode (piece 3). The prefill piece timing still prices the prompt side; there is no prompt excess left to recover in this pair.

## What a set containing an experts attempt with `min_rows: 2` would predict

**Prediction** (not measured): experts piece effect with `min_rows: 2` (piece 5: 0 / −1.386 / −6.574 ms/step at 1/8/32 decode rows vs the compiled reference) added to the pair's **measured, whole-request** decode change (piece 3, Claude's run; the swarm's anchor differs by ≤0.23 ms/step and would move each cell by that much). Prompt is not added but identical: every prompt-sized input is handed off in both routing (>32 rows) and experts (>59 rows) and combine's prefill code is unchanged, so the set's prompt time is the pair's. Whole-request prediction = pair's measured request change + steps × experts decode effect (steps 248/248/246/232 from `muse-vllm.jsonl`), i.e. prompt and decode compose as they did for the pair. The three-way term (gap 2) is **excluded** from the main cells and shown as a sensitivity row — per-slot wins are not assumed to compose.

Sensitivity to gap 2, if the historical three-way term recurs unchanged: add +2.13 to +2.86 ms/step at 8×128 (decode vs base becomes −0.03 to +0.70 ms/step; the set then fails G3 at 8×128 on decode and likely on whole request), +0.26 at 1×128 (inside the base spread historically; −0.90→−0.64, still a win, but the term was not claimed at 1), +0.19 at both 32-row workloads (−7.40→−7.21, −7.22→−7.03; immaterial against a 6.6 ms experts effect). Prefill sensitivity is **unverified**: no run has timed a three-slot prompt on the piece basis; the set's prompt cells above assume the hand-offs make it the pair's, which holds kernel-by-kernel but not against an unknown composition term.

Reading for the lead: additively, the set passes the compiled bar by a wide margin at 8/32 requests and exactly by the pair's margin at 1 request (and at 32×1024 the experts effect dwarfs gap 1's 0.36 ms problem). The whole prediction therefore stands or falls on gap 2 at 8×128 — the one workload where the prize (−1.39 ms/step) and the historical interaction (+2.13–2.86) are the same size. The fallback if the triple loses is the routing+combine pair, whose measured position is pieces 2–3.
