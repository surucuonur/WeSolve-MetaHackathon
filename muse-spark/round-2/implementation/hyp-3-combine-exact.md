### What must stay identical — bit for bit, contraction-free (changed)

- **Tolerance removed.** Round 1 allowed every element within 2 bfloat16 ULP (ULP floored at `|r| = 0.001`); now every element must equal the reference bit for bit (`differ == 0` in every layer at all six sizes; `claude/muse-agent-brief.md:186-188`; `bench/grader/check_candidate.py:90-99,139-150,172-175,308`). `max_ulp` is diagnostic only (`bench/grader/check_candidate.py:84-99`). Combining the two bfloat16 roundings — e.g. adding `shared` in float32 and rounding once — is therefore no longer acceptable: it changes the reference arithmetic and fails on any element where the intermediate rounding mattered (retires round-1 boundary line 50's allowance).
- **The exact evaluation sequence (new, load-bearing).** The reference (`bench/grader/slots.py:51-52`) runs eagerly as separate kernels, and a replacement must reproduce that sequence bit for bit (`muse-spark/round-2/exploration/territory-baseline-combine.md` checklist B–D; `muse-spark/round-2/exploration/ineff-baseline-combine-1.md:20-43`):
  1. cast `y` bfloat16→float32 exactly (bf16 ⊂ f32; `copy_v`);
  2. each product `p_j = float(y[r,j,d]) · weights[r,j]` rounded **once** to float32 (the `binary_g` multiply kernel, `Multiply` is plain `x * y` — the product may not stay unrounded into the next add);
  3. fold the 4 products as **((p₀+p₁)+p₂)+p₃** with a rounding after **every** add (the `col_reduce_small` sum: `Sum` is plain `a + b`, init 0, folded `t = p₀; t = p₁+t; t = p₂+t; t = p₃+t`; IEEE addition commutes, so this is exactly the left fold);
  4. round the sum **once** to bfloat16 (the `.astype`, `copy_v`, RNE);
  5. add `shared` in bfloat16 with a single rounding (`binary_vv` add).
  No reassociation (no pairwise/tree fold such as `(p₀+p₂)+(p₁+p₃)`), no fused multiply-add contraction at any step, and no other reordering is free any more. The fix the design names — suppress contraction, e.g. `metal::precise` operations or explicit rounding barriers, kernel otherwise unchanged (this entry, *Inefficiency*: "each product rounded once to float32, folded `((p₀+p₁)+p₂)+p₃` with a rounding after every add, one bfloat16 round, then the bfloat16 add of `shared`") — is the only arithmetic change allowed; thread/layout decomposition stays free.
- **The reference's kernels do not change with the row count (new).** Unlike the experts slot, combine has no 60-row switch: its 5 eager kernels (`copy_v`, broadcast `binary_g` multiply, strided-small `col_reduce_small` sum, `copy_v`, `binary_vv` add; `muse-spark/round-2/exploration/territory-baseline-combine.md` checklist B) are identical at every row count, the reduced axis is always size 4 (more rows only add threadgroups / non-column reductions), and the association in step 3 therefore does not change with `rows` (territory checklist C). The 60-row `gather_qvm → gather_qmm_rhs` switch belongs to the experts slot and cannot enter here, since the grader feeds captured `y` (territory checklist D; `bench/grader/check_candidate.py:277-304`). No `rows`-branched arithmetic is required — or permitted to differ: one evaluation sequence must hold at decode and prefill sizes alike, and a fix exact only at decode sizes fails at 2,048 rows (territory checklist D, folded grader note).
- **Tie rule and the order of the 4 pairs (kind unchanged, now exact).** No selection happens in this slot, so the 4th/5th-expert tie choice still belongs to routing only (round-1 boundary). What changed is that it is no longer insulated by tolerance: the `weights` this slot receives already embody the reference's tie choice, and combine must pair `weights[r,j]` with `y[r,j,:]` strictly positionally at every size — no re-sorting, re-picking or re-normalising experts, and no reordering of the 4 pairs beyond the fold in step 3. `weights` must still be treated as arbitrary finite float32: reference values are non-negative and sum to 2.5 per row, but a fused end-to-end model may feed anything finite (round-1 boundary, *Before (2)*).
- **Starting point against this boundary.** Checked line by line, `muse-spark/kernels/combine/attempt-1` keeps the shape/dtype/layout contract and both bfloat16 rounding points (`spec.json`: grid `rows*768`, threadgroup [256,1,1], inputs `y, weights, shared`, output `out` [rows,768] bfloat16; `block-0.metal:25`: `bfloat(acc) + shared[sbase]`), pairs positionally (`block-0.metal:16-23`), and writes the sum in the reference's left-fold order (`block-0.metal:20-23`). It fails this boundary on contraction only: compiled as a single Metal kernel, the compiler FP-contracts the mul+add chain into FMAs, keeping each product unrounded into its add, so `acc` can differ from the reference fold by ~1 float32 ULP and flip the bfloat16 rounding at a midpoint (`muse-spark/round-2/exploration/ineff-baseline-combine-1.md:15-43`). Measured under the current grader it differs at five of six sizes — decode 1 row 0 (pass), 8 rows **2** (max 2.0 ULP), 32 rows **2** (1.0 ULP); prefill 127 rows **6** (1.0), 1,016 rows **50** (16.0), 2,048 rows **130** (32.0), summed over 20 layers (`results/runs/2026-11-04-muse-round-1/grade-exact-combine.json`; territory checklist D table) — a roughly constant 3–16 ppm of elements, the same rounding-boundary lottery sampled more times at prefill, not a kernel switch. Round 1's CPU emulation proved this is contraction, not association (all 120 pure association trees give ≤1.0 ULP at 8 rows with zero fingerprint matches; the FMA-chain evaluation reproduces the fingerprint (0.0, 2.0, 1.0) exactly; `muse-spark/implementation/hyp-3-fused-combine.md`, *The 2.0-ULP touch*), and attempt-2's explicit `acc += …` loop shares the fingerprint, so neither loop form nor staging avoids it — only suppressing contraction while keeping the fold and both roundings can close it (`ineff-baseline-combine-1.md:34-43`).

### attempt-2 (`muse-spark/round-2/kernels/combine/attempt-2`) — boundary-3 · 2026-11-04T04:02:01Z — **pass**

Checked against the Boundary above for what G2 cannot see (static audit of `block-0.metal` and `spec.json`; no new grader run — the grader is a builder-only GPU job, and G2 already passed at all six sizes). No defect found.

- **Row counts other than 1, 8, 32, 127, 1,016, 2,048 — both sides of 60.** No graded value is special-cased and there is no `rows`-dependent branch at all: `row` is `threadgroup_position_in_grid.x` (`block-0.metal:20`), the grid is `rows*192` with one 192-thread threadgroup per row (`spec.json`), and every index is linear in `row` (`row*4`, `row*768`, `row*3072`; `block-0.metal:27,32,50,65`). Combine has no 60-row kernel switch (Boundary, *What must stay identical*), and none is introduced: untested decode 2–7 and 9–31 rows, prefill chunks of 33–126, 128–1,015, 1,017–2,047 and >2,048 rows, and later chunks all execute the identical per-row code path with only the threadgroup count changing (6,144 threadgroups at 2,048 rows). The per-element arithmetic is row-count-independent by construction, so exactness at the six graded sizes extends to every `rows ≥ 1` by the same argument the builder's 5×2,048-row random stress probes support empirically (`notes.md`). All offsets stay in 32-bit `uint` for any realistic chunk (`row*3072` overflows only beyond ~1.4M rows, far outside the slot's decode/prefill domain).
- **Unwritten threadgroup reads.** None. `wsh[4]` is written by `tid < 4` (`block-0.metal:26-28`) and `shsh[768]` is written exactly once per element — thread `tid` writes `shsh[tid*4 .. tid*4+3]` for `tid` in 0..191, covering 0..767 with no gap or overlap (`block-0.metal:31-39`) — and the single `threadgroup_barrier(mem_threadgroup)` at line 40 separates both write phases from every read (`wsh[0..3]` at lines 42–45, `shsh[base+k]` at line 83, where `base+k` is the reading thread's own staged quad). No other threadgroup location is read.
- **Threadgroup memory vs the 32 KB limit.** Not close: `wsh` 4×4 B + `shsh` 768×4 B = 3,088 B (9.4% of 32,768 B; `block-0.metal:23-24`), row-count independent. Threads per threadgroup are 192 of at most 1,024 (`spec.json`).
- **Thread-timing dependence.** None. The only cross-thread data flow is the staged `wsh`/`shsh`, ordered by the one barrier above; the volatile round-trips (`block-0.metal:70-82`) are thread-local register/stack operations; each output element `out[row*768 + tid*4 + k]` is written by exactly one thread, the `(row, tid, k)` partition is disjoint and covers all `rows*768` outputs, `atomic_outputs` is false and no atomics are used (`spec.json`). No reduction order can vary with scheduling — the fold is a fixed sequential chain within one thread.
- **Captured-input assumptions.** None beyond the slot contract. The kernel reproduces the Boundary's 5-step sequence by construction at every size: bf16→f32 by exact `<<16` expansion (`block-0.metal:35-38,59-62`), each product `yv[j][k] * wj` in its own statement forced through a volatile round-trip so it is rounded once to f32 before any add (`block-0.metal:69-76`), the left fold `((p₀+p₁)+p₂)+p₃` with a volatile rounding after each partial sum (lines 78–82; the final add needs no barrier — it is a lone f32 add with no multiply to contract into), then `bfloat(acc)` and the bf16 add of shared as two separate roundings (line 83; the staged `shsh` float is the exact f32 expansion of the bf16 value, so `bfloat(shsh[…])` recovers it bit for bit). Pairing is strictly positional — `wsh[j] = weights[row*4+j]` multiplies expert `j`'s quad from `yp[j*192 + tid]` into output columns `tid*4+k` (lines 42–54, 65–83) — with no re-sorting, re-normalising or expert re-picking. `weights` is treated as arbitrary finite f32 (no non-negativity or sum-to-2.5 assumption), and the `uint64_t` vectorised loads assume only the contract's row-contiguous layout and MLX's 8-byte-aligned allocations (row strides 1,536 B for `shared` and 6,144 B for `y` are multiples of 8 at every row). Hard-coded constants are the slot's fixed dimensions (768 columns, 4 experts, 192 threads), not properties of the captured arrays; other prompts, later decode steps, non-reference weights from a fused routing kernel, and rounding-midpoint or `shared ≈ −acc` cancellation inputs take the same path, and any NaN produced propagates as in the reference (the grader counts NaN-vs-NaN as equal, `check_candidate.py:93`).

### attempt-1 (`muse-spark/round-2/kernels/combine/attempt-1`) — boundary-3 · 2026-11-04T04:03:14Z — **pass**

Checked `block-0.metal` and `spec.json` against the Boundary above for what G2 cannot see (static audit; no new grader run — the grader is a builder-only GPU job, and G2 already passed at all six sizes, differ 0 / max ULP 0.0, `beats_compiled` true, as recorded under builder-3-a). No defect found.

- **Other row counts.** No value of `rows` is special-cased, or even read: the body has no `rows` branch, threshold or tail case (`block-0.metal:25-41`), and `spec.json` sets the grid to `rows*768` with one thread per output element. From `idx = thread_position_in_grid.x`, `row = idx / 768u` and `d = idx - row * 768u` give `row ∈ [0, rows)` and `d ∈ [0, 767]` for every `rows ≥ 1`, and because 768 = 3 × 256 the grid is always a whole number of 256-thread threadgroups. The maxima stay in bounds at any row count: `ybase + 2304 ≤ rows*3072 - 1`, `wbase + 3 ≤ rows*4 - 1`, `sbase ≤ rows*768 - 1`. Ungraded decode 2–7 and 9–31 rows therefore take exactly the graded path, as do prefill chunks of 33–126, 128–1,015, 1,017–2,047 and >2,048 rows and later chunks of any size: each output depends only on its own `(row, d)`, with no chunk state, cross-row grouping or rows-dependent arithmetic, matching the Boundary's row-invariant reference fold (the `col_reduce_small` association does not change with `rows`). The builder's ungraded probe additionally ran this kernel shape at 512 rows (3 × 393,216 random elements, differ 0; `attempt-1/notes.md:52-55`).
- **Volatile-barrier semantics.** The seven thread-local `volatile float` objects (`p0`–`p3`, `s01`, `s012`, `acc`, `block-0.metal:33-39`) are each written once and read once, in program order, so every product and every partial sum is materialised as a rounded float32 before the next operation can load it. The two contraction opportunities in this shape — folding `p2` or `p3` unrounded into `s012`/`acc` as an FMA — both have to cross a volatile load of the product and of the running sum, as does `s01 = p0 + p1`; the final `bfloat(acc) + shared[sbase]` (`block-0.metal:41`) contains no multiply to contract. The written fold is exactly `((p0+p1)+p2)+p3` with a rounding after every add (Boundary steps 2–3), followed by the single bfloat16 round and the bfloat16 `+ shared` (steps 4–5); there is no reassociation, no `fma`, and the two bfloat16 roundings are not merged. Evidence on the shipping toolchain (MLX 0.32.4, M1 Pro): differ 0 over (1+8+32+127+1,016+2,048)×768×20 = 49,643,520 graded element comparisons, where the unbarriered starting point differs 190 times over the same population, plus the builder's random-input probes above. Residual, not a defect: this rests on the Metal compiler honouring a volatile thread-local store/load as a rounding materialisation — MSL gives no named FP-contraction guarantee for `volatile`, and the literal `precise float` spelling does not compile under MLX's header (`attempt-1/notes.md:46-52`) — so exactness should be re-graded after any MLX or macOS toolchain change.
- **Timing dependence; unwritten threadgroup reads; GPU limits.** None. There is no threadgroup memory at all (0 of 32,768 B), no `threadgroup_barrier`, no atomics (`atomic_outputs: false`, `spec.json`), no simdgroup reduction and no shared state: the `volatile` objects are thread-private, each `out[sbase]` is written by exactly one thread (`idx` is a bijection onto outputs), and each thread's result depends only on its own reads. No scheduling order, warp timing or threadgroup interleaving can change a bit. Threads per threadgroup are 256 of at most 1,024 (`spec.json`), rows-independent like everything else in the kernel.
- **Arbitrary weights and other captured-input assumptions.** None beyond the slot contract. `weights[wbase + j]` is used once, positionally against `y[ybase + 768j]`, with no normalisation, clamping, reordering, sign or sum-to-2.5 assumption and no value-based branch (`block-0.metal:29-41`): negative, zero, very large or very small finite weights take the same seven rounded operations as the captured routing weights, so midpoint sums and the `shared ≈ −acc` cancellation case (where the reported ULP gap magnifies) reproduce the reference bit for bit by construction rather than by sampling — including the separate `bfloat(acc)` round the retired round-1 merge would have skipped. The only hard-coded constants are the slot's fixed dimensions (768 columns, 4 experts, 3,072 = 4×768), and `float(y[…])` is the exact bf16→f32 widening the reference's `copy_v` performs.
